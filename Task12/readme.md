2) Customers with month-to-month contracts show significantly higher churn than those with long-term contracts, highlighting the stabilizing effect of contract commitment.

3) Higher average monthly charges are associated with increased churn, suggesting price sensitivity among customers.

4) Customers in the low charge band tend to retain better than those in medium and high charge bands.

5) Long-tenure customers exhibit lower churn rates, indicating that customer lifetime value increases with retention duration.

6) Age group segmentation helps clearly identify high-risk churn segments, enabling targeted retention strategies.

7) Contract type emerges as a stronger churn driver than demographic variables such as gender.
